Built at SHAD 2025 at Memorial University in St. John's. Our team designed PulseRide, a rickshaw-inspired electric three-wheeler for low-occupancy rides in downtown Vancouver, built to stay efficient at downtown driving speeds.

I calculated the RPM band the motor needed to stay in at those speeds, then built a working mini-prototype using CAD-modelled, 3D-printed parts and custom circuit boards.

Partway through, the gearbox started failing under vibration. The housing was flexing and pulling the gears out of alignment, so I redesigned it. I also learned KiCad to clean up the prototype's PCB layout. The prototype won Best Prototype.

Alongside the prototype we wrote a business plan for running PulseRide as a ride service. In it we estimated the full-size vehicle would use about 6.3 kWh per 100 km, roughly 2.6 times more energy-efficient than a Tesla Model 3.
